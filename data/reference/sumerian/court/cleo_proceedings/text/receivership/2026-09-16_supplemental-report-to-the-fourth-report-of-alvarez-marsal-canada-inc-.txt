CNRL CURE COST CLAIM

7.      On September 11, 2026, CNRL submitted a proof of claim to the Receiver (the
        “CNRL Claim”). The CNRL Claim is a Cure Cost Claim in the amount of
        $88,233.15, representing amounts owing from Cleo to CNRL prior to the
        Receivership Date (the “Pre-Filing Balance”). A copy of the CNRL Claim is
        attached to this Report as Appendix “A”.

8.      CNRL did not submit a proof of claim to the Receiver prior to March 31, 2026 (the
        “Claims Bar Date”) because it believed the Pre-Filing Balance would be satisfied
        by 2698902 Alberta Corporation (the “269 Corp.”)2, rather than through the Claims
        Procedure on the basis that:

                a)   CNRL had entered into a settlement agreement with 269 Corp. on
                     October 17, 2025 (“Settlement Agreement”), pursuant to which 269
                     Corp. agreed to pay the Pre-Filing Balance in full; and

                b) when 269 Corp. failed to pay the amounts owing under the Settlement
                     Agreement, CNRL sought and obtained an order from this Court on
                     January 16, 2026 (the “Consent Order”), directing 269 Corp. to pay
                     CNRL, among other things, the Pre-Filing Balance. A copy of the
                     Consent Order is attached to this Report as Appendix “B”.

9.      CNRL informed the Receiver that it submitted the CNRL Claim 164 days after the
        Claims Bar Date because 269 Corp. failed to comply with its obligations under both
        the Settlement Agreement and the Consent Order, and CNRL's efforts at

      enforcement were unsuccessful. As a result of its inability to collect the Pre-Filing
      Balance from 269 Corp., CNRL is now seeking to receive a distribution of the same
      from the Purchase Price Funds.

10.   On September 11, 2026, the Receiver issued a notice of disallowance to CNRL (the
      “Notice of Disallowance”), which is attached to this Report as Appendix “C”. The
      Notice of Disallowance disallowed the CNRL Claim on the basis that:

             a)   it was submitted to the Receiver on September 11, 2026, which is 164
                  days after the Claims Bar Date of March 31, 2026; and

             b) it relates to amounts owed to CNRL by 269 Corp., and not ResidualCo,
                  pursuant to the Settlement Agreement and the Consent Order.

11.   The Receiver understands CNRL will be submitting a Notice of Dispute to the
      Receiver in response to the Notice of Disallowance. CNRL has indicated that it
      intends to oppose the proposed distribution being sought at the Receiver’s
      September 16, 2026, application (the “September 16 Application”) on the basis
      that the CNRL Claim meets the test for the admission of a late claim established by
      the Court of Appeal of Alberta in Enron Canada Corp. v National Oil-Well Canada
      Ltd., 2000 ABCA 285, namely:

             a)   Was the delay caused by inadvertence and if so, did the claimant act in
                  good faith?

             b) What is the effect of permitting the claim in terms of the existence and
                  impact of any relevant prejudice caused by the delay?

             c)   If relevant prejudice is found, can it be alleviated by attaching
                  appropriate conditions to an order permitting late filing?

             d) If relevant prejudice is found which cannot be alleviated, are there any
                  other considerations which may nonetheless warrant an order
                  permitting late filing?


                                            5
12.   The Receiver is of the view that the CNRL Claim does not meet the test for the
      admission of a late claim based on the following:

             a)   the delay was not caused by an inadvertence. CNRL was aware of the
                  Claims Procedure and did not submit a claim (or a placeholder claim)
                  until 164 days after the Claims Bar Date;

             b) permitting the CNRL Claim would result in the Pre-Filing Balance
                  being paid in its entirety from the Purchase Price Funds, which would
                  reduce the proposed ResidualCo Funds Distribution to the
                  Municipalities and the AER by approximately $88,000;

             c)   the prejudice to the Municipalities and the AER cannot be alleviated by
                  attaching conditions to an order permitting late filing; and

             d) in light of the facts outlined in the Notice of Disallowance, there are no
                  other considerations that would warrant an order permitting late filing.

13.   The Receiver is providing this information for the Court’s consideration and
      direction with respect to the proposed distribution sought at the September 16
      Application.
