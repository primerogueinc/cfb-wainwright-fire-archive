Grounds for making this Application:

Background

3.    On June 2, 2025, the Court granted a receivership order (the “Receivership Order”),
      whereby, effective June 3, 2025, the Receiver was appointed receiver and manager of
      CLEO pursuant to section 243(1) of the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act, RSC 1985, c B-
      3 (the “BIA”) and section 13(2) of the Judicature Act, RSA 2000 c J-2, thereby
      commencing these proceedings (the “Receivership Proceedings”).

4.    Prior to the granting of the Receivership Order, on December 8, 2024, the Company filed
      a Notice of Intention to Make a Proposal pursuant to section 50.4(1) of the BIA (the “NOI
      Proceedings”). Alvarez & Marsal Canada Inc. acted as Trustee during the NOI
      Proceedings (in such capacity, the “Proposal Trustee”).

5.    During the NOI Proceedings, this Honourable Court granted various Orders, including: (i)
      an Order approving a sales and solicitation process (the “SSP”) which set out how
      CLEO’s property would be marketed and sold in the NOI Proceedings; (ii) an Order
      approving the asset sale transaction to IHH Energy Corp., Nuova Strada Ventures Ltd.,
      and Surge Energy Inc. on March 25, 2025; and (iii) an Order approving a further asset
      sale transaction to Nuova and Rise Energy SPV Ltd. on June 2, 2025.

6.    It was CLEO’s intention to sell the majority of its assets during the NOI Proceedings.

7.    Due to the timing of the closing of the Court approved sale transactions, and the deemed
      bankruptcy date approaching under the NOI Proceedings, it was determined it would be
                                               -4-


      in the best interests of all parties for the Receiver to be appointed over the remaining
      property and assets of CLEO in order to close the Court-approved transactions in the
      Receivership Proceedings.

8.    Upon the application of uCapital-ULoan Solutions Inc., the interim lender during the NOI
      Proceedings, the Receivership Order was granted.

9.    The assets that were not sold in the transactions approved in the NOI Proceedings
      include properties located in Taber, Neutral Hills – South, Enchant, Fabyan, Shorncliffe,
      Atlee, Hayter, Kessler and Greater Wainwright (the “Remaining Assets”).

The SSP and Remarketing Process

10.   CLEO’s property was extensively marketed by the Company and its sale advisor during
      the NOI Proceedings in accordance with the SSP.

11.   On July 28, 2025, with the support of the Alberta Energy Regulator (“AER”), the
      Receiver initiated a brief remarketing process (the “Remarketing Process”) in an effort
      to sell the Remaining Assets.

12.   On August 29, 2025, the Receiver received 8 non-binding bids from interested parties
      (the “Non-Binding Bids”).

13.   Following the Remarketing Process deadline, the Receiver engaged with each of the 8
      bidders regarding additional due diligence requests and provided the AER with an
      update on the outcome of the Remarketing Process, including sharing copies of certain
      bids received on a confidential basis.

14.   After reviewing the Non-Binding Bids, in consultation with the AER, the Receiver
      selected the Purchaser as the successful bidder and executed the SPA.

The Proposed Transaction and Reverse Vesting Structure

15.   The Transaction contemplated in the SPA is a “reverse vesting” transaction which
      involves the Purchaser’s purchase and subscription, and the Receiver’s sale, of the
      common shares in CLEO.

16.   The essential terms of the SPA include:
                                               -5-


      (a)     the total cash consideration paid by the Purchaser for the Transaction is the
              Purchase Price, plus or minus the net amount of adjustments for production
              revenues earned and associated costs incurred by the Receiver from the
              Effective Date up to and including the Closing Date;

      (b)     CLEO shall retain the Retained Assets, Retained Liabilities and Retained
              Contracts;

      (c)     the Purchaser will purchase and subscribe to 200,000,000 common shares (the
              “Shares”) of CLEO, for a subscription amount equal to the Purchase Price; and

      (d)     the Purchaser’s purchase of the Shares, the Retained Assets, Retained
              Liabilities and Retained Contracts is on an “as is, where is” basis;

17.   The Shares and assets being conveyed pursuant to the SPA were sufficiently exposed
      to the market in a commercially reasonable and fair marketing process.

18.   The price to be paid for the Shares pursuant to the SPA represents the highest and best
      price that can be obtained for the assets, property and undertakings of CLEO in the
      current circumstances.

19.   The SPA, as proposed, is in the best interests of the Company’s estate and its
      stakeholders.

20.   The Purchaser is not a current licensee with the AER, which necessitates the RVO
      structure to complete the Transaction.

21.   Given the time, cost, expense and uncertainty surrounding an asset sale transaction, the
      Purchaser is not willing to purchase the Remaining Assets and assume CLEO’s liabilities
      pursuant to any other structure other than an RVO.

22.   There is no other viable transaction in respect of the undertakings, asset and property of
      CLEO.

Approval of the RVO

23.   The issuance of the RVO is a condition precedent to the closing of the SPA. The SPA
      contemplates that the Purchaser will own all of the issued and outstanding Shares. To
      give effect to this, the Excluded Assets, the Excluded Liabilities and the Excluded
                                               -6-


      Contracts must be transferred to ResidualCo in order to ensure there are no Excluded
      Liabilities, Excluded Contracts and Excluded Assets remaining in CLEO.

24.   The RVO is necessary to carry out the Transaction so as to avoid the necessity of AER
      license transfers and any delays and value deterioration that such transfers may cause.
      The Receiver is of the respectful view that the Receivership proceedings is the
      appropriate proceeding to grant the RVO and that any other proceedings, such as a
      CCAA Proceedings, would not be feasible or practical as a result of the circumstances
      presented in the receivership proceedings.

25.   All of the Excluded Assets, the Excluded Liabilities and the Excluded Contracts shall be
      transferred to ResidualCo. The Losses and Encumbrances shall attach to the Excluded
      Assets.

26.   The SPA represents the highest and best offer for the assets being sold and does not
      result in the transfer of any environmental liabilities and abandonment and reclamation
      obligations to the Orphan Well Association, thereby achieving the best possible outcome
      for CLEO’s stakeholders under the circumstances.

27.   The Receiver is not aware of any stakeholders who would be worse off as a result of the
      RVO structure.

The Modified RVO Addresses the Court’s Concerns with Cure Costs

28.   On October 17, 2025, the Receiver brought an application to approve the SPA and
      Transaction.

29.   At the hearing, the RVO was objected to by Alberta Energy and Minerals (“Alberta
      Energy”), as the Transaction did not contemplate the payment of pre-filing amounts
      owing by CLEO to the Crown in right in Alberta, despite Crown mineral leases remaining
      with CLEO.

30.   At the October 17, 2025 hearing, the Court directed parties to submit written arguments
      for their respective positions, and on October 28, 2025, the Court released its Reasons
      for Decision (“Reasons”), dismissing the Receiver’s application for the RVO, without
      prejudice to its ability to apply again for approval of the proposed Transaction.
                                               -7-


31.   In the Reasons, the Court notes that the use of reverse vesting orders allows contracts
      to be retained without payment of cure costs, which is a curtailment of the principle of
      freedom of contract.

32.   The Receiver has worked with counsel to Alberta Energy to modify the RVO to include
      paragraph 10.1, which ensures that any validly owing cure costs, including those owing
      to the Crown in right of Alberta, are satisfied from the Purchase Price proceeds, or
      alternatively, any contract counterparties owed cure costs are permitted to terminate
      their agreement with the Company. This proposed revision to the RVO is to ensure the
      Transaction structure will not curtail the principle of freedom of contract.

33.   The Receiver has shared the modified form of RVO with key stakeholders who were in
      attendance and made submissions at the October 17 hearing, including counsel to
      Alberta Energy, counsel to the AER, counsel to the OWA, Department of Justice
      Canada, and counsel to certain municipalities. The Receiver has not been made aware
      of any of these parties having concerns with the modified form of RVO. A blackline of the
      modified form of RVO to the form of RVO that was attached to the Application for the
      October 17 hearing is at Schedule “C”.

34.   Since the amount of cure costs owing, if any, to counterparties of contracts that will be
      assumed by the Purchaser is unknown, the Receiver will review the books and records
      of the Company and return to Court to seek approval of a claims process in respect of
      any cure costs that may be owing. A claims process will be necessary to determine
      claims and priorities to each pool of funds, being the proceeds to be transferred into the
      estate of ResidualCo from previously approved transactions of CLEO’s assets, and the
      remaining funds available from the Purchase Price.

35.   All known contract counterparties that may be owed cure costs are included on the
      service list in these Receivership Proceedings.

37.   The Released Parties are necessary and essential to the restructuring of CLEO.
