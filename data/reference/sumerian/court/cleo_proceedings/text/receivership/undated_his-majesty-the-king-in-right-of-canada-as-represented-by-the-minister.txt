PART I – OVERVIEW

   1. The Receiver seeks declarations regarding Wage Earner Protection Program
      (“WEPP”) entitlement.

   2. To do so, the Receiver requests this Court to declare that:


               (a) the Reverse Vesting Order granted November 14, 2025 (“Revised RVO”)
               does not impact previously approved claims or payments made to former
               employees of Cleo Energy Corp. under the Wage Earners Protection Program
               Act1 ( “WEPPA” ); and

               (b) an employee's entitlement under the WEPPA is determined at the time of
               termination and is not altered by the subsequent granting of the Revised RVO.

   3. The Receiver is thereby seeking that this Court determine the impact of the Revised
      RVO on the WEPP applications of the terminated employees.

   4. The Attorney General of Canada, on behalf of Employment and Social Development
      Canada, opposes the declarations sought.

   5. The Receiver's proposed declarations would determine the legal effect of the
      Revised RVO on the individual former employees' entitlement to payments under a
      federal statutory benefits program, notwithstanding that the Minister has already
      made determinations under the WEPPA concerning those employees.

   6. The statutory avenue of review and appeal is determined under the WEPPA. The
      precise legal issue that the Receiver asks this Court to determine, whether the
      Revised RVO altered the employees' entitlement under the WEPPA, is one that
      Parliament has assigned to the Minister and, on appeal, to the Canada Industrial
      Relations Board (“CIRB”).

   7. Pursuant to the WEPPA,the Minister has sole and exclusive authority to determine
      whether an individual is eligible to receiver WEPP payments. Where an applicant
      disagrees with the Minister’s decision, the legislation provides a specific review and
      appeal mechanism to the CIRB.

   8. We respectfuly submit that this Honourable Court should decline to issue the
      declaration on the basis that the Superior Court lacks jurisdiction to make the
      declaration, or alternatively should decline to make the declaration in light of the
      prescribed review and appeal provisions set out in the WEPPA which has been

PART II – STATEMENT OF FACTS

    9. On June 2, 2025 this Court granted a receivership order appointing Alvarez & Marsal
       Canada Inc. as receiver and manager of all the property of Cleo Energy Corp.
       (“Cleo”).

    10. The Receiver terminated the employees of Cleo shortly after the Receivership Order
        was issued.

    11. The terminated employees (the “WEPP Applicants”) applied to the WEPP in
        respect of outstanding wages owed at the time of their termination. The WEPP
        Applicants received WEPP payments.

    12. The Receiver brought an application to approve a transaction through a reverse
        vesting order structure. That application was heard by Justice Feasby on October
        17, 2025. Justice Feasby reserved his decision and provided parties the opportunity
        to provide additional written submissions, which ESDC and the Receiver provided 2.

    13. Pursuant to a written decision dated October 28, 2025, Justice Feasby denied the
        application but provided leave for the Receiver to reapply. A further application for
        a sale by reverse vesting order was made by the Receiver, which was heard by
        Justice Bourque on November 14, 2025.

    14. The Court issued a reverse vesting order for the sale of the shares of Cleo on
        November 14, 2025 and on the closing of that transaction Cleo exited the
        Receivership Proceedings and was replaced by 2755857 Alberta Ltd.

    15. After Cleo exited the Receivership Proceedings, the Minister reconsidered the
        previous eligibility determinations of the WEPP Applicants and determined that
        they were not eligible for payments from the WEPP. The WEPP Applicants and the
        Receiver were informed of the redetermination decisions of the Minister.

    16. The WEPP Applicants all exercised their statutory right to seek a review of the
        Minister’s determinations pursuant the WEPPA.

   23. Should declarations sought by the Receiver be made by the Court?


PART IV – LAW AND ARGUMENT

   24. The WEPPA was enacted to establish a federal program, the WEPP. It is designed to
       allow the federal government to make timely payments to former employees who
       are owed eligible unpaid wages by an insolvent employer and who meet the
       eligibility requirements determined by the statute. Its purpose is to provide relief to
       individuals whose employment has been terminated by an insolvent employer who
       ceases or will cease to operate its business operations.

   25. In a receivership, the employee eligibility requirements to receive WEPP payments
       are set out in s. 5(1) of the WEPPA, which stipulates that the following conditions
       must be met:

          a. the person’s employment has ended for a reason prescribed by s. 3 of the
             WEPPR (WEPPA, s. 5(1)(a));
                                                     -6-



            b. the former employer is subject to a receivership (WEPPA, s. 5(1)(b)(ii)); and,

            c. the individual is owed eligible wages by their former employer (WEPPA, s.
               5(1)(c)).

        These conditions are determined under the exclusive jurisdiction of the Minister3,
        which determination is subject to review by the Minister and to an appeal before the
        CIRB.4 The Board’s decision is subject to judicial review under the exclusive
        jurisdiction of the Federal Court of Appeal.5

    26. The WEPPA does not require any Superior Court involvement where the former
        employer is subject to a receivership. Unlike a proceeding under the CCAA or
        Division I of the BIA, where a declaration from the Court is required prior to former
        employees applying to the Program, no such application is needed in the case of a
        receivership.

    27. The Minister has exclusive jurisdiction pursuant to the WEPPA to make WEPP
        eligibility determinations.6

    28. The Minister must independently assess if eligibility conditions under s. 5(1) of the
        WEPPA are satisfied, as the Quebec Court of Appeal acknowledged in Former
        Gestion.7

    29. The Receiver describes the question before the Court as “determining whether the
        Revised RVO had any impact on the eligibility of former employees of Cleo for
        entitlement to payments under the Wage Earner Protection Program Act.”8

    30. The declarations that the Receiver is urging the Court to make on plain reading seek
        to impact the independence of the Minister in assessing the criteria under section
        5(1) of the WEPPA by dictating the result that the Receiver believes is appropriate.
        The declarations sought seek to dictate the legal effect of the RVO transaction on
        the “eligibility” of applicants, and also to define the time parameters that the
        Minister must examine and evaluate such a claim.

Principles on receivers seeking advice and directions in the form of declaratory relief

    31. Where a trustee, or in this case a receiver, seeks advice and direction for
        declaratory relief or declaratory judgments there are a number of principles that
        have developed through the jurisprudence including:

             •       applications for advice and directions are appropriate to ensure the proper
                     and timely administration of the estate;9

             •       a court should decline to provide advice and directions on matters that do
                     not directly relate to the receivership;10

             •       a court should decline to provide advice and directions when the advice and
                     direction would substantively impact proceedings in another jurisdiction
                     before another court which are properly before the court in the other
                     jurisdiction;11

             •       a court should not issue a declaratory order that would interfere with the
                     jurisdiction assigned to a specialized tribunal;12

             •       a court should decline to provide directions which has no practical
                     utility;13and,

             •       a court should decline to make a declaration where the declaration would be
                     useless or embarrasing or where an adequate alternative alternative remedy
                     is available;14

    32. None of the principles listed above support the declarations sought by the Receiver.

    33. The declarations requested do not facilitate the administration of the receivership,
        concerns a matter falling within the Minister’s statutory jurisdiction under the
        WEPPA, and seeks to address issues for which a specific statutory review process
        already exists and has been pursued. Moreover, the requested declaration would
        have no practical utility, as the Minister has already rendered decisions regarding
        the WEPP Applicants’ eligibility and review requests.

    34. The Alberta Court of Appeal in the case of Canada (Attorney General) v. Ernst &
        Young Inc.15 allowed an appeal of a declaration that was issued in a receivership.
        The Receiver had sought and obtained a declaration from the court that a
        disclaimer of interest did not create a new defence for the Attorney General who
        was involved in separate litigation with other stakeholders. In allowing the appeal,
        the Court held that there was was no basis to conclude that the receiver was
        entitled to the declaration it sought, and that the Chambers Judge had erred in
        relying on the Receivership Order as authority to grant the declaration.

    35. In the current receivership, the Receiver has not identified anything in the
        Receivership Order that would entitle it to the declarations. In these circumstances,
        the Court should decline to exercise its advisory jurisdiction or grant the declaratory
        relief sought.

The Court should not make the declaration sought on the impact of the Revised RVO on the
claims of the WEPP Applicants

        dislike of the possibility that previous payments to the WEPP Applicants could be

        and that recourse from such a decision was to the CIRB and the Federal Court of

Adequate alternative remedy for terminated employees to appeal the Minister’s decision

   42. The WEPP Applicants did not need to seek any court declaration within the
       receivership to apply for WEPP, nor was such an application made (or required to be
       made) by the Receiver.

   43. The WEPPA sets out a specific mechanism for an applicant to dispute an eligibility
       decision they are dissatisfied with. Initially they have a right to request a Ministerial
       review of the decision, which review can confirm, rescind or vary a determination20.
       If dissatisfied with the result of that review Parliament has set out a prescribed

       mechanism for an applicant to follow to dispute the decision, namely an appeal to
       the CIRB.21

   44. The WEPP Applicants have already engaged in the review process under WEPPA.
       Each WEPP Applicant sought review of the redetermination decision. The decisions
       were confirmed by the Minister. They have a clear path available for them should
       they want to contest the Minister’s redeterminations.

   45. The WEPPA does not contemplate bringing an application to the Superior Court
       dealing with the insolvency to contest a decision of the Minister, and in fact strictly
       prohibits a court from questioning or reviewing that decision.

   46. Section 13 of WEPPA22 provides:

       Review is final
       13 Subject to the right of appeal under section 14, the Minister’s confirmation,
       variation or rescission, as the case may be, is final and may not be questioned or
       reviewed in any court.

   47. Given the engagement of the WEPP Applicants in the administrative review process
       and the specific restrictions set out in section 13 of WEPPA, the Court should
       decline to make the declarations sought.

The Court should not make the declaration sought on the timing an employee’s entitlement
under WEPPA is to be determined

   48. There is no specific provision in the WEPPA that specifies how and when the
       Minister is to consider an applicant’s entitlement to the payments from the WEPP.

   49. Leave to appeal was granted by the Quebec Court of Appeal23 in the insolvency of
       Valeo Pharma Inc. where one of the central questions is in respect of timing of
       terminations for the purposes of WEPPA.

   50. In that case, some but not all employees of Valeo were to be transferred to a
       residual co following an RVO transaction, and other employees had their
       employment continue with Valeo. The transaction contemplated that the
       employees transferred to residual co would be terminated in residual co and would
       then apply for benefits under WEPPA. The Monitor sought an obtained an order
       from the Quebec Court that the residual co was a former employer within the
       meaning of para 5(5) of the WEPPA and that the criteria prescribed by s. 3.2 of the

   51. The Quebec Court of Appeal heard the appeal in Valeo on September 30, 2025, and

   52. The declaration on the timing of eligibility falls squarely in the Minister’s jurisdiction
       in respect of the WEPP Applicants. Moreover, the Minister has made a decision,
       and as noted above the WEPP Applicants have pursued the review process provided
       by WEPPA, and the Minister has provided decisions on the reconsideration
       requests.

   53. The declaration sought by the Receiver is one that appears to be prohibited by s. 13
       of WEPPA as in essence it is being sought by the Receiver from the Court in
       response to the Minister’s decision, and for no other apparent purpose.

The declarations sought do not have any purpose within the receivership

   54. The declarations sought by the Receiver do not appear to have a purpose in the
       receivership itself.

   55. The Receiver’s Fourth Report and the accompanying Brief are clear that the
       Receiver is of the opinion that the Minister’s redetermination decision on
       entitlement is not correct. However there is nothing in the Application filed by the
       Receiver, the Receiver’s Fourth Report nor the Brief filed by the Receiver that sets
       out what the declarations it seeks are intended to do within the receivership of
       2755857 Alberta Ltd, or otherwise, or how such declarations are needed for the
       Receiver to complete its duties.

   56. The WEPP Applicants have followed the mechanism set out in the Act, namely they
       sought a review of the decisions by the Minister. The decisions were reviewed and
       the decisions were confirmed.

   57. The path to dispute the review decision is to appeal the decision(s) to the CIRB. This
       is not only an adequate alternative mechanism, but the only proper one under
       WEPPA which provides that “subject to the right of appeal under section 14, the
       Minister’s confirmation, variation or rescission as the case may be, is final and may
       not be questioned or reviewed in any court.”

   58. Appeals to the CIRB are on the record and no new evidence is admissible24. Given
       the statutory restriction, and the fact that the Ministerial review decisions have

PART V – CONCLUSION

   59. The Court does not have jurisdiction to make the declarations sought, or
       alternatively should decline to make the declarations sought.

   60. In the context of the current receivership, the declarations are not required by the
       Receiver to fulfil any of its duties in administering the estate to its conclusion. It is
       unclear what utility the declarations the Receiver seeks will have.

   61. The WEPP Applicants have already taken steps to dispute the ministerial
       determinations regarding their eligibility, and have a clear path to appeal such
       decisions if they choose to do so. The CIRB and the Federal Court of Appeal each
       are capable of reviewing the factual matrix and determining such appeals.

PART VI – RELIEF SOUGHT

   62. The Crown asks that this Court decline to issue the declarations sought concerning
       the legal effect of the Revised RVO and on the timing of eligibility.
