REMEDY CLAIMED OR SOUGHT:

   1. An Order allocating the Receiver’s Charge and Receiver’s Borrowings Charge amongst
      the various assets comprising the Property.

   2. An Order requiring the Receiver to pay to the Receiver General of Canada, from the
      proceeds of Cleo Energy Corp.’s (“Cleo”) assets, $899,907.51 in satisfaction of the
      unremitted GST held in trust by Cleo on behalf of His Majesty the King in Right of
      Canada as Represented by the Minister of National Revenue, (the “Crown”), himself as
      represented by the Canada Revenue Agency (“CRA”).

   3. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court may deem just.

GROUNDS FOR MAKING THIS APPLICATION:

Background

   4. The CRA has filed a $1,355,296.64 GST claim with the Receiver, of which $899,907.51
      is subject to a statutory deemed trust.

   5. The Receiver has already paid CRA’s payroll deemed trust claim pursuant to subsection
      227(4.1) of the Income Tax Act, RSC 1985 c 1 (5th Supp).

   6. The First Report of the Receiver filed within this proceeding did not address payment of
      the CRA’s GST deemed trust claim, nor have they paid it.

CRA’s GST Deemed Trust in the Receivership Context

   7. CRA’s priority to the proceeds of sale of the assets of Cleo in respect of the GST deemed
      trust, pursuant to subsection 222(3) of the Excise Tax Act, has the same priority as the
      payroll deemed trust.

   8. The GST deemed trust survives and applies in receivership proceedings unless and until
      the debtor is bankrupt (per subsection 222(1.1) of the Excise Tax Act).
                                                                                       Page 3 of 5


   9. Cleo is not bankrupt nor will it become bankrupt in these proceedings; therefore, the GST
      deemed trust remains operative.

   10. CRA is entitled to be paid it’s GST deemed trust amount of $899,907.51 from the sale
      proceeds before any funds are transferred.

Allocation of Receiver’s Charge and Receiver’s Borrowings Charge

   11. According to paragraph 21 of the First Report of the Receiver, the June 2025
      Transactions generated approximately $2.1 million in sales proceeds (the “Sale
      Proceeds”).

   12. From the Sale Proceeds, the Receiver made payments of $1,295,342 (the “Priority
      Payments”), leaving approximately $804,658 in Sale Proceeds unallocated.

   13. In addition to the Sale Proceeds, the Receiver also received $1,193,341 of Oil & Gas
      Revenue and $43,501 of other income (the “Other Proceeds”).

   14. As of the First Receiver’s Report, the Receiver had an ending cash balance of $990,788.
      After accounting for the Sale Proceeds not explicitly allocated, the Receiver retained
      $186,130 of the Other Proceeds.

   15. As of the Second Receiver’s Report, the Receiver retained an ending cash balance of
      $791,396 as of October 31, 2025.

   16. If the Revised RVO transaction closes, the Receiver will also have the Purchase Price
      paid by the purchaser (the “Purchase Proceeds”), the amount of which is currently
      confidential.

   17. The Receiver’s election to make the Priority Payments from the Sale Proceeds, rather
      than exhausting the Other Proceeds and the Purchase Proceeds first limits the amount
      available to satisfy the CRA’s GST deemed trust claim, while increasing the amount that
      will be available to transfer to ResidualCo. for the benefit of the unsecured creditors.
                                                                                       Page 4 of 5


   18. Given the nature of CRA’s GST deemed trust claim and its priority over unsecured
       creditors, the Minister requests that this Court allocate the Priority Payments to be made
       first from the Other Proceeds, the Purchase Proceeds and then from the Sale Proceeds.
