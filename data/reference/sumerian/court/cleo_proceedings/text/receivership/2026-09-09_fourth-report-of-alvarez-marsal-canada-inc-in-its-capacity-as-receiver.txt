INTRODUCTION

1.   On June 2, 2025, the Court of King’s Bench of Alberta (the “Court”) granted a
     receivership order (the “Receivership Order”), whereby, effective June 3, 2025
     (the “Receivership Date”), Alvarez & Marsal Canada Inc. (“A&M”) was
     appointed receiver and manager (the “Receiver”), without security, of all the
     current and future assets, undertakings and properties of every nature and kind
     whatsoever, and wherever situate, including all proceeds thereof (collectively, the
     “Property”) of CLEO Energy Corp. (“Cleo” or the “Company”), pursuant to
     section 243(1) of the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act, RSC 1985, c B-3 (the “BIA”)
     and section 13(2) of the Judicature Act, RSA 2000 c J-2 (the “Receivership
     Proceedings”).

2.   Prior to the granting of the Receivership Order, on December 8, 2024, the Company
     filed a Notice of Intention to Make a Proposal pursuant to section 50.4(1) of the
     BIA (the “NOI Proceedings”). A&M acted as Trustee under the Proposal (in such
     capacity the “Proposal Trustee”) during the NOI Proceedings.

3.   The Receivership Order empowered and authorized, but did not obligate, the
     Receiver to, among other things, manage, operate and carry on the business of the
     Company and to take possession and control of the Property (as defined in the
     Receivership Order) and any and all proceeds, receipts and disbursements arising
     out of or from the Company, and to sell, convey, transfer, lease or assign the
     Property or any part or parts thereof out of the ordinary course of business.

Reversal of WEPP Payments

22.   On October 17, 2025, the Receiver applied to the Court for approval of, among
      other things, a reverse vesting order transaction (the “Transaction”) pursuant to
      which, among other things, all of the outstanding shares of Cleo would be sold to a
      third-party purchaser and ResidualCo would replace Cleo in the Receivership
      Proceedings as the insolvent party (the “October 17 Application”). On November
      14, 2025, the Court approved the Transaction, and on November 19, 2025, the
      Receiver closed the Transaction following the satisfaction of all material conditions
      precedent to closing, as set out in the SPA.

23.   At the October 17 Application, the Department of Justice Canada appeared on
      behalf of ESDC and advised the Court that, as a result of the structure of the
      Transaction and Cleo’s subsequent exit from the Receivership Proceedings, ESDC
      may seek to claw back the WEPP Payments made to the Former Employees.



                                           11
24.   In response to the Department of Justice’s comments at the October 17 Application,
      the Receiver advised the Court that, in its view, the Transaction and the resulting
      exit of Cleo from the Receivership Proceedings should not affect the Former
      Employees’ entitlement to the WEPP Payments. The Former Employees met all
      eligibility requirements under the WEPPA at the time the WEPP Payments were
      made, and the Transaction simply monetized the remainder of Cleo’s assets for the
      benefit of the estate. The Transaction is consistent with the goals and purposes of
      insolvency proceedings and, in the Receiver’s view, any attempts by ESDC to seek
      repayment from the Former Employees would be contrary to the purposes of the
      WEPPA. The Receiver also complied in remitting the Super Priority Amount to the
      ESDC.

25.   The Court addressed the potential reversal of the WEPP Payments in its reasons for
      decision dated October 28, 2025 (the “Reasons for Decision”), stating at paragraph
      46: “A choice to clawback payments made to terminated employees of an insolvent
      corporation because of subsequent events beyond their control would be to the
      immense discredit of the Minister and, I would hope, be swiftly reversed by the
      Canada Industrial Relations Board or the Federal Court of Appeal”.

26.   Between March 2026 and April 2026, the Receiver became aware through certain
      Former Employees that ESDC had chosen to claw back the WEPP Payments made
      to the Former Employees, notwithstanding the Court’s comments on WEPP
      Payments in the Reasons for Decision discussed above. In certain cases, ESDC,
      through the CRA or otherwise, has caused the tax returns of the Former Employees
      to be withheld until repayment of the WEPP Payments were made. The Receiver
      understands that certain Former Employees applied to have ESDC’s decision
      reversed; however, the Receiver is not aware of any such reversals having been
      issued as of the date of this Report.

Relief Sought in Respect of WEPP Payments

27.   The Receiver is of the understanding that the position of the Department of Justice
      is that this matter cannot be resolved by this Honourable Court, but instead must be

                                              12
      adjudicated by the Canada Industrial Relations Board or Federal Court of Canada.
      The Receiver is of the respectful view that as this is a proceeding in the Court of
      King’s Bench, and as the Revised RVO, an order of this Court, was relied upon to
      reverse or nullify the WEPP entitlements, it is appropriate to seek the advice and
      direction from this Court as to the implication of the Revised RVO on the Former
      Employee’s WEPP claims.

28.   As more particularly set out in the Receiver’s Brief of Law filed in support of the
      Application, recent jurisprudence has addressed the impact of a reverse vesting
      order in WEPP entitlements to employees. In those decisions, courts have
      determined that the time for determining entitlement for WEPP is at the termination
      of employment, and further a residual corporation is a “former employer” under the
      WEPPA.

29.   In light of the recent decisions of the Courts in Ontario and Quebec and having
      regard to the comments made by the Court in the Reasons for Decision, the
      Receiver seeks a declaration that the Revised RVO did not impact any WEPP
      entitlements of the Former Employees, and the ESDC is not entitled to retroactively
      claw back the WEPP Payments.

30.   The Receiver is of the respectful view that such a declaration is fair, appropriate in
      the circumstances and is consistent with the goals and purposes of insolvency
      proceedings and the WEPP. The Former Employees were terminated as a result of
      Cleo’s receivership and therefore satisfied the applicable eligibility requirements
      under the WEPPA at the time the WEPP Payments were made. In addition, the
      Receiver shares the same view that was outlined in the Court’s comments (as
      discussed above) within the Reasons for Decision. The subsequent reversal of the
      WEPP Payments arose solely from the Court-approved Transaction in the
      Receivership Proceedings and the resulting substitution of ResidualCo for Cleo.

39.   The Receiver, in consultation with Miller Thomson, has conducted a review of the
      Secured Pre-Filing Claims to determine the priority of the respective Claimants
      with respect to the ResidualCo Funds. Based on the Receiver’s review, it is unclear
      whether the Municipalities or the AER has priority over the ResidualCo Funds, as
      each may have priority pursuant to different legislative provisions.

40.   For example, pursuant to Section 103 of the Oil and Gas Conservation Act
      (Alberta), the AER has a lien in respect of any costs, levy, fee, or penalty, among
      other things, owing to the AER, and which is stated to take priority over all other
      liens.




                                              18
41.   However, pursuant to Sections 348 and 348.1 of the Municipal Government Act
      (Alberta), the Municipalities have a lien for certain taxes owing which is stated to
      take priority over all other liens, except those in favour of the Crown.

42.   The matter is further complicated by Section 4 of the Responsible Energy
      Development Act (Alberta), which says that the AER is not an agent of the Crown,
      however recent Court authority (such as Orphan Well Association v. Trident
