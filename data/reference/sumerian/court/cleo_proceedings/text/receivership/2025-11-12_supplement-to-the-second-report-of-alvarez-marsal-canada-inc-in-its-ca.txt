INTRODUCTION

1.   On June 2, 2025, the Court of King’s Bench of Alberta (the “Court”) granted a
     receivership order (the “Receivership Order”), whereby, effective June 3, 2025
     (the “Receivership Date”), Alvarez & Marsal Canada Inc. (“A&M”) was
     appointed receiver and manager (the “Receiver”), without security, of all the
     current and future assets, undertakings and properties of every nature and kind
     whatsoever, and wherever situate, including all proceeds thereof (collectively, the
     “Property”) of CLEO Energy Corp. (“Cleo” or the “Company”), pursuant to
     section 243(1) of the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act, RSC 1985, c B-3 (the “BIA”)
     and section 13(2) of the Judicature Act, RSA 2000 c J-2 (the “Receivership
     Proceedings”).

2.   On November 5, 2025, the Receiver filed its Second Report to the Court (the
     “Second Report”) in support of its Application scheduled for November 14, 2025
     (the “November 14th Application”), that provided, among other things:

             a)     a brief summary of the Reasons for Decision regarding the proposed
                    RVO and the potential clawback of Wage Earner Protection Program
                    payments;

             b) a summary of the proposed reverse vesting transaction contemplated in
                    the Share Purchase Agreement (the “SPA”) between Cleo and 2698902
                    Alberta Corporation (“269” or the “Purchaser”) dated October 6, 2025
                    (the “Transaction”); and

             c)     the Receiver’s recommendation on the SPA and the revised reverse
                    vesting order (“Revised RVO”).

GST DEEMED TRUST

DoJ’s Position on the GST Deemed Trust

9.    The DoJ has taken the position that the CRA has priority to the proceeds from the
      sale of Cleo’s assets in respect of the GST Deemed Trust, and that, pursuant to
      subsection 222(3) of the Excise Tax Act, this priority is equivalent to that of the
      payroll deemed trust in the amount of $70,346.84 (the “Payroll Deemed Trust”).
      On that basis, the DoJ asserts that the CRA is entitled to payment of the GST
      Deemed Trust from the sale proceeds, prior to the transfer of any funds to
      ResidualCo upon closing of the proposed Transaction.

10.   The DoJ and the CRA have not sought to lift the stay of proceedings that is in place
      over the property of Cleo pursuant to paragraphs 8 and 9 of the Receivership Order
      with respect to the DoJ Application.



                                             5
Receiver’s Review of Deemed Trust Priorities

11.   The Receiver reviewed the Payroll Deemed Trust and determined that it ranked in
      priority to other creditors. As such, the Receiver paid the Payroll Deemed Trust on
      September 17, 2025.

12.   The Receiver has not completed a security review or obtained a priority opinion in
      respect of the alleged GST Deemed Trust. The Receiver is of the view that payment
      of the GST Deemed Trust, at this time, may prejudice other creditors, including
      those who may wish to seek a bankruptcy order to determine the appropriate
      ranking of creditor claims under BIA. In addition, the Receiver has been unable to
      confirm the validity and quantum of the alleged GST Deemed Trust and requires
      additional time to compare this claim to the Company’s books and records.

13.   The Receiver and its counsel have consulted with certain other creditors and their
      respective counsel, including parties holding statutory priority claims (such as other
      Crown stakeholders), and the Receiver is advised that they are not supportive of
      the DoJ Application. A letter from external legal counsel for Paintearth County,
      Flagstaff County, the M.D. of Wainwright, and Vulcan County (collectively, the
      “Municipalities”) opposing the DoJ Application is attached to this Report as
      Appendix “A”.

14.   In the letter, the Municipalities requested that the Receiver bring a bankruptcy
      application. The Receiver requires additional time to consider this request and to
      consult with stakeholders. Any hearing relating to distributions or matters that
      impact priorities should proceed on full notice to the service list. The Receiver
      therefore proposes that the proposed Transaction be approved and permitted to
      close, with stakeholder distribution applications to be addressed in a future
      application before the Court.

15.   Following further stakeholder consultation, the Receiver intends to seek a
      subsequent order authorizing it to either conduct a claims process or bring the
      bankruptcy application requested by the Municipalities. This will follow further

RE:      Receivership of Cleo Energy Corp. (“Cleo”) | Court File No. 2501-09028

We are legal counsel for Paintearth County, Flagstaff County, the M.D. of Wainwright, and
Vulcan County (the “Municipalities”) with respect to the above-noted matter, and are writing in
relation to the application by the Canada Revenue Agency (“CRA”) returnable November 14,
2025.

The CRA is seeking an order which, if granted by the Court of King’s Bench, would require the
Receiver to pay the sum of $899,907.51 to the CRA, in satisfaction of Cleo’s unremitted GST
amounts. If this amount were paid to the CRA, we understand that would effectively reduce the
proceeds of sale available in the receivership to nil, and leave all secured creditors, including
the Municipalities, with essentially no prospect of recovery.

For reference, the amounts owing by Cleo to each Municipality for outstanding taxes are as
follows:

    •    Vulcan County - $93,554.28, current to November 2025
    •    M.D. of Wainwright - $1,920,415.75, current to January 2025
    •    Flagstaff County - $493,385.57, current to November 2025
    •    Paintearth County - $388,248.17, current to November 2025

The Municipalities observe, and the CRA has admitted in their application materials, that their
claim constitutes a statutory deemed trust pursuant to the Excise Tax Act. Pursuant to section
67 of the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act, such deemed trusts are treated only as an ordinary
unsecured claim in a bankruptcy context. The CRA’s request for relief, accordingly, hinges
entirely on the fact that Cleo has not yet been declared a bankrupt.




1387-6738-4346, v. 2
In light of the above, the Municipalities oppose the CRA’s application, and respectfully request
that the Receiver apply to the Court of King’s Bench for authorization to immediately assign
Cleo into bankruptcy.

In support of this request, the Municipalities note that Cleo’s receivership has, since its
commencement earlier this year, been treated as a liquidation. Since the NOI process was
converted to a receivership, there has been essentially no prospect of a successful proposal.
Accordingly, in the Municipalities’ view, it would be contrary to the spirit and intent of the
legislative scheme to allow the CRA’s application to proceed unhindered. In essence, the CRA
is seeking a distribution in a manner that would be contrary the scheme of priorities established
by the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act.

Please confirm whether the Receiver would be amenable to the above. If so, the Municipalities
will support the Receiver in its application for authorization to assign Cleo into bankruptcy.
