I.       INTRODUCTION

1.       This Reply Brief of Law is filed by Alvarez & Marsal Canada Inc., in its capacity as Court-
         appointed receiver and manager (the “Receiver”) of the undertakings, properties and
         assets of CLEO Energy Corp. (“Cleo”) in response to the letter of the Department of
         Justice Canada (“DOJ”) dated October 22, 2025 on behalf of Employment and Social
         Development Canada (“ESDC”).1

2.       ESDC claims that the transaction (the “Transaction”) contemplated by the share
         purchase agreement (the “SPA”) between the Receiver and 2698902 Alberta Corp.
         pursuant to a reverse vesting order (“RVO”) will retroactively disqualify Cleo’s former
         employee’s, who were terminated by the Receiver in the receivership proceeding, from
         the payments received by such employees pursuant to the Wage Earner Protection
         Program (“WEPP”).

3.       ESDC’s position is unfounded. Eligibility for WEPP is determined at the time of
         termination, and the RVO has no impact on the WEPP payments made to Cleo’s former
         employees.

4.       The factual background is set out in the First Report of the Receiver dated October 6,

         Report.

5.       A summary of the key facts as they relate to the reply is set out in the following section.

15.     WEPP is established to provide payments to individuals in respect of wages owed to them
        by employers who are insolvent.13

16.     Section 5(1) of the WEPPA outlines that a former employee may receive payment if
        (among other reasons):

        (a)     the individual’s employment ended for a reason prescribed by regulation;

        (b)     the former employer is subject to a receivership; and

        (c)     the individual is owed eligible wages by the former employer.14

17.     The regulations specify that an individual’s employment has ended under section 5(a) of
        WEPPA if the individual’s employment has terminated.15

18.     As described by the Superior Court of Quebec in Arrangement relatif à Former Gestion
        Inc., WEPPA is “clearly remedial legislation intended to provide protection to workers who
        suffer lost wages as a result of a bankruptcy or restructuring.”16 Due to its remedial nature,
        WEPPA should be “given such, fair, large and liberal construction and interpretation as
        best ensures the attainment of its objectives.”17 The mischief sought to be cured by
        WEPPA is the absence of a solvent employer who can pay wages owing to former
        employees.18

19.     Arrangement relatif à Former Gestion Inc. was a case where the debtor group sold their
        assets to a purchaser under both an approval and vesting order and a reverse vesting

20.         Regardless of whether an insolvent former employer sells its assets pursuant to an asset
            purchase transaction, or it sells its shares pursuant to a reverse vesting transaction, the
            employees have no solvent employer from which to claim wages.21 Since the object of
            WEPPA is to protect terminated employees who lost wages in their former employer’s
            bankruptcy or restructuring, interpreting WEPPA to prevent those employees’ recoveries
            runs contrary to its purpose.22

21.         The relevant time of determination of employees’ entitlements under WEPPA is “the
            moment at which an insolvent entity’s employees are terminated due to a bankruptcy or
            restructuring”—what happens after that moment is irrelevant.23

22.         The Superior Court of Quebec further stated that, in the event an employee is owed eligible
            wages by a former employer, at the moment the former employer terminated all its
            employees, s. 5.1(c) of WEPPA is satisfied.24

23.         Cleo’s situation is analogous to the situation in Arrangement relatif à Former Gestation
            Inc. Cleo’s former employees made claims under WEPP for unpaid wages following their
            termination during an insolvency proceeding. The circumstances of the employees do not
            change as a result of the Transaction. The former employees remain unemployed and
            unpaid by Cleo. and thus are entitled to WEPP.

24.         As noted above, WEPP is established to provide payments to individuals in respect of
            wages owed to them by employers who are insolvent. The suggestion that the RVO will
            have any retroactive impact on the “eligibility criteria” of the employees undermines “the
            objective of the WEPP”, is factually inaccurate, and is contrary to the policy objectives of
            WEPP itself.

25.         The RVO should not impact any eligibility requirements of any former employees of Cleo
            to WEPP. There is no public interest served by a claw back on the WEPP funds or claims.

V.    CONCLUSION

28.   There is no basis in fact, law, policy or equity for ESDC to seek reimbursement from former
      employees of Cleo for WEPP payments issued as a result of such employees’ termination
      by the Receiver in these Receivership proceedings.

29.   Commercial courts have made it clear that the relevant time of determination of
      employees’ entitlements under WEPP is “the moment at which an insolvent entity’s
      employees are terminated due to a bankruptcy or restructuring”. This is consistent with
      the stated purpose of the program.

30.   The Transaction does not change the circumstances of the former employees of Cleo and
      the RVO should include clarifying language that it does not reverse any WEPP eligibility
      requirements of these former employees.
