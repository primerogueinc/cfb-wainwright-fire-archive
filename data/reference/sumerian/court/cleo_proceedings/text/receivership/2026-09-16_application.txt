Grounds for making this Application:

Background

3.    On June 2, 2025, the Court granted a receivership order (the “Receivership Order”),
      whereby, effective June 3, 2025, the Receiver was appointed receiver and manager of
      Cleo Energy Corp. (“Cleo”) pursuant to section 243(1) of the Bankruptcy and Insolvency
      Act, RSC 1985, c B-3 (the “BIA”), and section 13(2) of the Judicature Act, RSA 2000 c J-
      2 (the “Receivership Proceedings”).

4.    Prior to the granting of the Receivership Order, on December 8, 2024, Cleo filed a Notice
      of Intention to Make a Proposal pursuant to section 50.4(1) of the BIA (the “NOI
      Proceedings”). A&M acted as Proposal Trustee (in such capacity, the “Proposal
      Trustee”) during the NOI Proceedings.

5.    During the NOI Proceedings, this Honourable Court granted various Orders, including: (i)
      an Order approving a sales and solicitation process which set out how Cleo’s property
      would be marketed and sold in the NOI Proceedings; (ii) Orders approving the asset sale
      transactions to IHH Energy Corp., Nuova Strada Ventures Ltd., and Surge Energy Inc. on
      March 25, 2025; and (iii) Orders approving a further asset sale transactions to Nuova
      Strada Ventures Ltd. and Rise Energy SPV Ltd. on June 2, 2025.

6.    It was Cleo’s intention to sell the majority of its assets during the NOI Proceedings.

7.    Due to the timing of the closing of the Court approved June 2, 2025 sale transactions, and
      the deemed bankruptcy date approaching under the NOI Proceedings, it was determined
                                               -4-


      it would be in the best interests of all parties for the Receiver to close the transactions in
      the Receivership Proceedings. On the application of Cleo’s interim lender, uCapital-ULoan
      Solutions Inc., the Receivership Order was granted.

8.    The assets that were not sold in the transactions approved in the NOI Proceedings include
      properties located in Taber, Neutral Hills – South, Enchant, Fabyan, Shorncliffe, Atlee,
      Hayter, Kessler and Greater Wainwright.

9.    On November 14, 2025, the Court approved a reverse vesting order (“Revised RVO”) for
      the shares of Cleo, which resulted in the sale of Cleo’s remaining properties, and vested
      in ResidualCo certain Excluded Liabilities and Excluded Contracts (as defined in the
      Revised RVO).

10.   The Revised RVO saw Cleo exit the Receivership Proceedings and have it replaced with
      ResidualCo.

11.   On February 10, 2026, the Court approved a claims procedure order (the “Claims
      Procedure Order”) to establish the quantum of: (i) pre-filing claims as at the Receivership
      Date that attach to the funds of ResidualCo; and (ii) other cure cost claims, excluding
      certain cure costs of the Alberta Crown, that attach to the purchase price funds (the
      “Purchase Price Funds”) from the transaction (“Cure Cost Claims”).

WEPPA

12.   Upon the granting of the Receivership Order all employees of Cleo were terminated
      (the “Former Employees”).

13.   The Former Employees with the assistance of the Receiver applied for the Wage Earner
      Protection Program (“WEPP”). These Former Employees were provided payments in
      accordance with their entitlements under the WEPPA (the “WEPP Payments”).

14.   Prior to granting the Revised RVO, Employment and Social Development Canada
      (“ESDC”) advised that any Former Employees who received the WEPP Payments might
      have the same clawed back as a result of the Revised RVO.

15.   Following the granting of the Revised RVO, ESDC issued notices to the Former
      Employees requiring the immediate return of the WEPP Payments.
                                               -5-


16.    The relevant time of determination of employees’ entitlements under WEPPA is the
       moment at which an insolvent entity’s employees are terminated due to a bankruptcy or
       insolvency.

17.    A clawback of the WEPP Payments following termination during the Receivership
       Proceedings due to the Revised RVO is unfair, improper and prejudicial to the Former
       Employees, and in contravention to the purposes of the WEPP.

18.    The remedial nature of WEPPA is lost with the clawbacks.

Distributions

19.    Pursuant to the Revised RVO, the Cure Cost Claims are entitled to be paid from the
       Purchase Price Funds, after payment of the Proven Alberta Crown Cure Costs, on a pro
       rata basis, subject only to the Receiver’s Charge.

20.    The Receiver, in consultation with Miller Thomson, has conducted a review of the Cure
       Cost Claims

21.    The Receiver proposes to make a distribution to the Cure Cost Claimants as set out in the
       Fourth Report.

22.    The Purchase Price Funds exceed the Cure Cost Claims and any remaining funds shall
       be transferred to the ResidualCo Funds.

23.    The Receiver, in consultation with Miller Thomson, has conducted a review of the Secured
       Pre-Filing Claims to determine the priority of the respective Claimants with respect to the
       ResidualCo Funds.

24.    The two creditors with statutory priority liens, being the AER and Municipalities, have
       negotiated a distribution amongst themselves wherein the parties shall receive a pro-rata
       distribution of all ResidualCo Funds remaining in the Receivership estate (the
       “Settlement”).

25.    The Receiver supports the distribution to the AER and Municipalities as set out in the
       Fourth Report and in accordance with the Settlement.
